# Sensory Profile Tool

## Purpose

A sensory profile documents how a student responds to different types of sensory input, identifying what they seek, avoid, or are sensitive to. This information guides environmental modifications and accommodations.

## Who it is for

- Students who show signs of sensory sensitivities or differences
- Anyone experiencing overwhelm in certain environments
- Learners with unexplained behaviour challenges that may have sensory roots
- Students who seek specific types of sensory input
- Professionals and families working to understand sensory needs

## How to use

1. **Observe across settings**: Watch how student responds to sensory input at school, home, and community
2. **Note patterns**: Record what sensory experiences the student seeks, avoids, or tolerates
3. **Document all sensory systems**: Consider visual, auditory, tactile, proprioceptive, vestibular, olfactory, gustatory, and interoceptive
4. **Include context**: Note when and where sensitivities or seeking behaviours occur
5. **Identify accommodations**: Based on profile, determine helpful supports and modifications
6. **Share with team**: Ensure all adults supporting the student understand sensory profile
7. **Update regularly**: Sensory needs may change over time

### Sensory systems to profile

**Visual**: Lighting, bright colours, visual clutter, movement, patterns
**Auditory**: Background noise, sudden sounds, specific frequencies, volume
**Tactile**: Touch, textures, clothing, temperature, water
**Proprioceptive**: Heavy work, pressure, resistance, body awareness
**Vestibular**: Movement, spinning, swinging, position changes
**Olfactory**: Smells from food, cleaning products, perfumes, environment
**Gustatory**: Food textures, tastes, temperatures
**Interoceptive**: Awareness of hunger, thirst, toileting, pain, emotions

### Categories to document

- **Seeks**: Sensory input the student actively pursues
- **Avoids**: Sensory input the student rejects or withdraws from
- **Sensitive**: Input that causes distress or overwhelm
- **Under-responsive**: Input student doesn't seem to notice or needs more of

## Notes

- Consult with occupational therapist for comprehensive assessment
- Sensory needs are real, not behavioural choices
- Accommodations should be available as needed, not earned
- What helps one student may distress another
- Sensory profiles guide environmental design and accommodation planning
- Respect student's sensory preferences as valid needs
