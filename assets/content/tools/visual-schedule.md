# Visual Schedule Tool

## Purpose

A visual schedule provides a clear sequence of activities, reducing anxiety and supporting independence. Visual schedules answer the question "What happens next?" using pictures, symbols, or words.

## Who it is for

- Students who experience anxiety about transitions or changes
- Learners who benefit from visual information over verbal instructions
- Anyone who struggles with time management or sequence understanding
- Students working toward increased independence

## How to use

1. **Choose format**: Select pictures, photographs, symbols, or written words based on student's comprehension level
2. **Determine scope**: Create schedule for full day, single period, or specific routine
3. **Arrange sequence**: List activities in order from top to bottom or left to right
4. **Make it visible**: Place schedule where student can easily see and reference it
5. **Teach use**: Show student how to check schedule and mark completed activities
6. **Update regularly**: Change schedule to reflect actual daily activities
7. **Reference frequently**: Refer student to schedule throughout day

### Schedule formats

- **Object schedule**: Actual objects representing activities (for pre-symbolic learners)
- **Photo schedule**: Photographs of activities, places, or materials
- **Symbol schedule**: Icons or line drawings representing activities
- **Written schedule**: Words or phrases listing activities
- **Combination**: Mixing formats to match student's learning needs

### What to include

- All major activities in sequence
- Transition times
- Preferred activities to motivate checking schedule
- Visual "finished" marker or check boxes
- Clear start and end points

## Notes

- Involve the student in creating the schedule when possible
- Balance predictability with flexibility - teach that schedules can change
- Use consistent format and location
- Don't remove schedule too quickly - independence may require ongoing visual support
- Consider portable mini-schedules for transitions between settings
- Celebrate schedule-checking as independence, not dependence
