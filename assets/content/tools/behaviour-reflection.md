# Behaviour Reflection Tool

## Purpose

A structured reflection tool reframes "behaviour" as communication, helping adults understand what a student is trying to communicate through their actions. This tool moves from punishment-focused to understanding-focused responses.

## Who it is for

- Teachers responding to challenging behaviour
- Support staff seeking to understand behaviour function
- Families trying to interpret their child's actions
- Teams conducting functional behaviour assessments
- Anyone moving toward neurodiversity-affirming behaviour support

## How to use

1. **Describe what happened**: Record observable behaviour without judgement
2. **Note what came before**: What was happening immediately before the behaviour? (antecedent)
3. **Note what came after**: What happened immediately following the behaviour? (consequence)
4. **Ask "What might they be communicating?"**: Reframe behaviour as an attempt to express a need
5. **Identify possible unmet need**: What need might be driving this communication?
6. **Consider context**: Sensory environment, social demands, task difficulty, energy level, wellbeing
7. **Plan support**: What could help meet the underlying need? What alternative communication could be taught?

### Behaviour as communication framework

**Instead of**: "They're being disruptive"
**Ask**: "What are they trying to tell us?"

**Possible messages**:
- "This is too hard"
- "I need a break"
- "I don't understand"
- "This is too loud/bright/overwhelming"
- "I need connection"
- "I'm in pain or uncomfortable"
- "I need to move"
- "I feel unsafe"

### Reflection questions

- What need might this behaviour be meeting?
- What is the student trying to communicate?
- What environmental factors might be contributing?
- What skills might be lagging?
- How could we change the environment to prevent this?
- What alternative, more effective communication could we teach?
- Are our expectations reasonable and appropriate?

## Notes

- Behaviour is always communication, even if the message isn't immediately clear
- Avoid labelling behaviours as "attention-seeking" - connection is a valid human need
- Consider sensory, social, academic, and emotional factors
- Focus on prevention and teaching, not punishment
- Involve the student in reflection when appropriate
- Remember that behaviour often results from lagging skills, not wilful non-compliance
- This tool supports neurodiversity-affirming, trauma-informed practice
