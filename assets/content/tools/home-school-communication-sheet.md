# Home-School Communication Sheet

## Purpose

A structured communication tool enabling consistent information sharing between home and school. Effective home-school communication ensures everyone supporting the student has the information they need and can work as a coordinated team.

## Who it is for

- Teachers and families sharing daily information
- SNAs communicating with parents about the school day
- Parents providing context about home situations affecting school performance
- Support teams coordinating consistent approaches
- Anyone working to bridge home and school environments

## How to use

1. **Choose format**: Paper form, email template, app, or communication book
2. **Determine frequency**: Daily, weekly, or as-needed depending on student needs
3. **Keep it manageable**: Brief, structured formats are more sustainable than lengthy narratives
4. **Focus on useful information**: Share what genuinely helps the other setting support the student
5. **Maintain positivity**: Always include positive observations alongside challenges
6. **Ensure consistency**: Agree who completes it and when
7. **Respond promptly**: Acknowledge and act on shared information

### Information to include (from school)

- Notable successes or achievements
- Specific challenges encountered
- Strategies that worked well today
- Changes to routine or upcoming events
- Sensory or regulatory observations
- Social interactions (positive and challenging)
- Academic progress or concerns
- Items needed from home

### Information to include (from home)

- Sleep quality and duration
- Appetite and eating patterns
- Significant home events affecting mood
- Sensory sensitivities currently heightened
- Strategies working well at home
- Concerns to monitor at school
- Medication changes
- Upcoming family events or changes

### Communication principles

- Brief and structured (not essays)
- Factual and objective
- Solution-focused, not blame-focused
- Timely - share information when it's useful
- Confidential - protect student privacy
- Collaborative - "we're on the same team"

## Notes

- Consistency matters more than perfection
- Don't wait for problems to communicate - share successes too
- Keep tone professional and collaborative
- Avoid jargon - use plain language
- Remember that parents are experts on their child
- School staff have expertise in educational contexts
- Both perspectives are valuable and necessary
- If communication feels one-sided, address it directly
- Consider using structured templates to make it easier
