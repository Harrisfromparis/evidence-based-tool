# Task Analysis

## What it is

Breaking complex skills or activities into small, sequential steps. Each step is taught individually, then chained together to form the complete skill.

## Why it matters

Complex tasks can be overwhelming. Task analysis makes skills teachable by identifying exactly what steps are needed and in what order, allowing systematic instruction and reducing frustration.

## How to implement

1. **Observe the skill**: Watch someone competent perform the task and note every step
2. **List steps sequentially**: Write down each step in the order it occurs
3. **Make steps specific**: Each step should be a single, observable action
4. **Teach systematically**: Teach one step at a time, or teach the whole chain with support
5. **Use visual supports**: Create visual task analysis (photos, drawings, checklists)
6. **Fade supports**: Gradually reduce prompts as student learns the sequence

### Teaching approaches

- **Forward chaining**: Teach first step independently, then add second step, etc.
- **Backward chaining**: Adult completes all steps; student does last step independently, then last two steps, working backwards
- **Total task teaching**: Student attempts all steps with prompting; prompts gradually reduced

### Practical examples

- Handwashing broken into 10 steps with visual sequence
- Getting ready for PE: shoes off → socks off → PE gear on → shoes on, etc.
- Science experiment procedure broken into sequential, numbered steps
- Morning arrival routine: hang coat, unpack bag, hand in homework, get book, sit at desk
- Making sandwich: gather materials → bread on plate → spread butter → add filling → cut → clean up

## Good to know

- **Individual differences**: Step size depends on student's ability level
- **Visual task analysis is powerful**: Photos or drawings of each step support independence
- **Backward chaining often works well**: Finishing independently provides natural reinforcement
- **Analyse your own performance**: Sometimes we discover 'hidden' steps we do automatically

## Evidence base

Task analysis is a cornerstone of behavioural teaching with extensive research support. Particularly effective when combined with visual supports, prompting hierarchies, and systematic reinforcement.

**Related practices**: Prompting, Visual Supports, Structured Work Systems, Discrete Trial Training
