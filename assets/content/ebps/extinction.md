# Extinction

## What it is

Withholding reinforcement for a previously reinforced behaviour, leading to decrease in that behaviour. Extinction involves no longer providing the consequence that was maintaining the behaviour.

## Why it matters

When used ethically and carefully, extinction can reduce behaviours that are maintained by attention or access to preferred items. However, extinction must be implemented with caution and functional understanding.

## How to implement

1. **Conduct functional behaviour assessment**: Ensure you understand what reinforces the behaviour
2. **Ensure safety**: Extinction is inappropriate if behaviour escalates dangerously
3. **Withhold reinforcement consistently**: If behaviour was reinforced by attention, provide no attention; if by access to items, do not provide access
4. **Expect extinction burst**: Behaviour often increases before decreasing
5. **Reinforce alternative behaviours**: Simultaneously teach and reinforce more appropriate ways to get needs met
6. **Be consistent**: Intermittent reinforcement makes behaviour more resistant to extinction

### Types of extinction

- **Attention extinction**: Withholding attention for attention-maintained behaviour
- **Tangible extinction**: Not providing items for behaviour maintained by access to items
- **Escape extinction**: Not allowing escape from demands for escape-maintained behaviour

### Important limitations and cautions

- **Never use alone**: Must be combined with teaching alternative appropriate behaviours
- **Extinction burst**: Behaviour typically gets worse before improving
- **Spontaneous recovery**: Behaviour may briefly return even after apparently extinguished
- **Ethical concerns**: Ignoring communication about genuine needs is harmful
- **Safety first**: If behaviour poses danger, extinction is inappropriate

## Good to know

- **Not appropriate for all behaviours**: Never use extinction for behaviour communicating genuine distress, pain, or safety concerns
- **Requires consistency**: Everyone must respond the same way, or behaviour becomes more persistent
- **Can increase distress**: Extinction can be frustrating and should be used carefully
- **Alternatives often better**: Teaching replacement behaviours is often more effective and respectful

## Evidence base

Extinction has strong research support when used appropriately with functional understanding and combined with teaching alternative behaviours. However, ethical concerns about potential distress mean it should be used cautiously and only when alternative approaches have been considered.

**Related practices**: Functional Behaviour Assessment, Differential Reinforcement, Functional Communication Training
