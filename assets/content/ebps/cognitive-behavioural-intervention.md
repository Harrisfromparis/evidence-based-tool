# Cognitive Behavioural Intervention

## What it is

Teaching students to recognise thoughts, feelings, and behaviours, and develop coping strategies. CBT-based approaches help students understand connections between their internal experiences and external responses.

## Why it matters

Many autistic students experience anxiety, overwhelm, and difficulty regulating emotions. CBT provides tools for identifying emotions, understanding triggers, and practising alternative responses that support wellbeing.

## How to implement

1. **Teach emotion identification**: Use visuals, scales, or body maps to help students recognise feelings
2. **Connect feelings to situations**: Help students identify patterns ("When X happens, I feel Y")
3. **Introduce coping strategies**: Teach one simple technique (deep breathing, taking space, using words)
4. **Practise when calm**: Rehearse strategies before they're needed, not during distress
5. **Reinforce use**: Notice and acknowledge when the student uses a coping strategy

### Practical examples

- Use a "feelings thermometer" to rate anxiety levels before tests
- Teach thought-challenging for social anxiety ("Everyone thinks I'm strange" → "Some people are friendly to me")
- Create a personal "calm-down plan" with drawings of helpful strategies
- Keep a mood diary to identify patterns between sleep, routine, and wellbeing
- Use comic strip conversations to understand social situations

## Good to know

- **Not for forcing conformity**: CBT should support wellbeing, not suppress valid responses to harmful situations
- **Respect protective behaviours**: Some behaviours are coping mechanisms, not irrational responses
- **Avoid invalidation**: Never use CBT to gaslight student experiences ("You're overreacting")
- **Student choice matters**: Participation must be genuinely voluntary

## Evidence base

CBT approaches have strong research support for anxiety, emotional regulation, and adaptive coping in autistic populations when appropriately modified for autistic communication and thinking styles. Effectiveness increases when approaches are concrete, visual, and respect autistic ways of processing.

**Related practices**: Self-Management, Social Narratives, Social Skills Training
