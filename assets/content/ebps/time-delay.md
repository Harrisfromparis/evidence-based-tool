# Time Delay

## What it is

Systematically waiting a specific number of seconds after an instruction or opportunity before providing a prompt. Time delay promotes independence by giving students opportunity to respond without immediate adult assistance.

## Why it matters

Immediate prompting can create prompt dependence. Time delay encourages students to initiate independently and reduces reliance on adult cues, building confidence and self-directed learning.

## How to implement

1. **Start with 0-second delay**: Initially provide prompt immediately (errorless learning)
2. **Introduce delay**: After student shows understanding, wait 3-5 seconds before prompting
3. **Increase gradually**: Extend delay if student needs more processing time (up to 10 seconds)
4. **Reinforce independent responses**: Provide strongest praise when student responds without prompt
5. **Provide prompt after delay**: If student doesn't respond within delay period, give planned prompt
6. **Be consistent**: Use same delay interval within teaching session

### Types of time delay

- **Constant time delay**: Same delay interval used throughout (e.g., always 4 seconds)
- **Progressive time delay**: Delay gradually increases (0 seconds → 2 seconds → 4 seconds → 6 seconds)

### Practical examples

- After asking "What's next?", teacher waits 5 seconds for student to check visual schedule before pointing to it
- During maths lesson, teacher presents problem and waits 4 seconds before offering help
- Adult says "Time to wash hands" and waits 3 seconds before gesturing toward bathroom
- Teacher gives instruction and pauses 5 seconds before repeating or prompting

## Good to know

- **Processing time varies**: Some students need more time than others to process and respond
- **Avoid excessive delay**: Waiting too long can lead to errors and frustration
- **Combine with other strategies**: Works well with visual supports and least-to-most prompting
- **Respect non-response**: Sometimes "no response" is a communicative choice, not just delayed processing

## Evidence base

Time delay has strong research support as an effective and efficient teaching procedure. Studies show it promotes independence, reduces prompt dependence, and is effective across diverse skills and populations.
