# Structured Work Systems

## What it is

Organising tasks, materials, and work environments to provide clear visual information about what work to do, how much, when it's finished, and what comes next. Structured work systems reduce ambiguity and support independence.

## Why it matters

Ambiguity and unpredictability create anxiety and reduce task engagement. Clear visual structure allows students to work independently, reduces reliance on adult prompts, and builds confidence through predictable routines.

## How to implement

1. **Organise space visually**: Clearly define work areas with physical boundaries or visual markers
2. **Provide visual schedules**: Show sequence of tasks to complete
3. **Structure materials**: Organise materials left-to-right or top-to-bottom to show work flow
4. **Clarify completion**: Make it obvious when a task is done (tick box, finished basket)
5. **Indicate what's next**: Show what happens after work completion (schedule, visual reminder)

### Key questions structured work systems answer

- **What work do I do?** (clear, labelled tasks)
- **How much work?** (visual quantity - 3 worksheets, 10 problems)
- **How do I know I'm finished?** (completion signal - last item, timer, checklist)
- **What do I do next?** (visual schedule shows next activity)

### Practical examples

- Left-to-right task baskets: work in left basket, completed work in right basket
- Visual checklist showing morning routine steps with tick boxes
- Numbered folders indicating sequence of assignments to complete
- Work folder system: To-Do, Doing, Done
- Visual timer showing time remaining for activity

## Good to know

- **Individual adaptation**: What works for one student may not work for another
- **Reduce verbal instructions**: Let the visual structure do the communication
- **Build independence gradually**: Start with adult support, fade to self-management
- **Useful for everyone**: Structured work systems benefit all students, not only autistic learners

## Evidence base

Structured work systems, particularly those based on TEACCH principles, have strong research support for increasing independence, reducing anxiety, improving task completion, and decreasing reliance on adult prompts.

**Related practices**: Visual Supports, Task Analysis, Self-Management
