# Collaborative Problem-Solving

## What it is

Working together with students to identify challenges, understand underlying concerns, and generate mutually acceptable solutions. CPS emphasises partnership over adult-imposed solutions and views challenging behaviour as lagging skills rather than wilful non-compliance.

## Why it matters

Traditional behaviour management often fails because it doesn't address the underlying reasons for difficulties. Collaborative problem-solving respects student perspective, builds problem-solving skills, and creates solutions that work for everyone involved.

## How to implement

### Three steps of collaborative problem-solving

**Step 1: Empathy**
Gather information about the student's concern or perspective
- "I've noticed you're having difficulty with..."
- Listen without judgement
- Understand the student's experience

**Step 2: Define the Problem**
Share adult concern and identify the problem to be solved together
- "The thing is, I'm concerned that..."
- Define how both perspectives create a problem to solve
- Ensure student understands adult concern

**Step 3: Invitation**
Invite student to collaborate on solutions
- "I wonder if there's a way..."
- Brainstorm solutions together
- Select mutually acceptable solution
- Try it out and review

### Practical examples

- Student refuses homework → Discussion reveals excessive evening activities leave no time → Solution: Reduce homework load or complete some during school
- Student leaves class frequently → Empathy uncovers sensory overwhelm → Solution: Scheduled breaks + discrete signal for emergency exit
- Student refuses group work → Discussion reveals peer conflicts → Solution: Choice of partners + clear role definitions

## Good to know

- **Not permissiveness**: CPS maintains expectations whilst finding workable solutions
- **Requires genuine partnership**: Adult must truly listen and value student input
- **Challenging at first**: Both adults and students need practice with this approach
- **Lagging skills, not won't**: Reframe "won't" as "can't yet" or "doesn't have the skills for this yet"
- **Reduces power struggles**: Collaborative approach eliminates need for control battles

## Evidence base

Collaborative problem-solving has growing research support showing reductions in challenging behaviours, improved relationships, development of problem-solving skills, and reduced need for restrictive interventions. Particularly effective for students whose behaviour stems from lagging skills rather than motivational issues.

**Related practices**: Cognitive Behavioural Intervention, Self-Management, Functional Behaviour Assessment
