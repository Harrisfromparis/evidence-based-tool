# Exercise and Movement

## What it is

Incorporating physical activity and movement breaks into the school day to support regulation, focus, and wellbeing.

## Why it matters

Movement supports sensory regulation, reduces anxiety, improves focus, and promotes physical health. Many autistic students have sensory-seeking needs or proprioceptive differences that are supported through intentional movement opportunities.

## How to implement

1. **Identify movement needs**: Observe when the student is most restless or unfocused
2. **Offer regular movement breaks**: Build brief activity into the schedule (every 30-45 minutes)
3. **Provide movement options**: Let students choose activities that meet their sensory needs
4. **Integrate movement into learning**: Use active learning strategies within lessons
5. **Normalise movement**: Allow fidget tools, standing, or movement during seated work

### Practical examples

- 5-minute movement breaks between subjects (jumping jacks, stretching, walking)
- Access to exercise equipment (resistance bands, small weights, yoga ball seating)
- "Brain breaks" with movement-based activities for whole class
- Delivery tasks that involve walking to other classrooms
- Standing desks or wobble cushions for students who benefit from movement
- Trampoline or outdoor time as part of sensory diet

## Good to know

- **Individual differences**: Some students are sensory-seeking and need lots of movement; others may find vigorous activity dysregulating
- **Movement is not punishment or reward**: It's a regulatory need, not something to be earned
- **Whole-class benefit**: Movement breaks support all students, not just autistic learners
- **Monitor for patterns**: Track whether movement improves focus and adjust timing accordingly

## Evidence base

Research consistently shows physical activity supports attention, reduces anxiety and challenging behaviours, and improves wellbeing for autistic individuals. Movement breaks are particularly effective when individualised to sensory profiles.

**Related practices**: Antecedent-Based Intervention, Self-Management, Sensory Integration
