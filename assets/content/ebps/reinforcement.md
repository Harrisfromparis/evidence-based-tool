# Reinforcement

## What it is

Providing consequences that increase the likelihood a behaviour will occur again. Reinforcement can be social (praise, attention), tangible (preferred items), activity-based (access to preferred activities), or sensory.

## Why it matters

Behaviour that is reinforced increases; behaviour that is not reinforced decreases. Strategic use of reinforcement helps students learn new skills and replace challenging behaviours with more effective ones.

## How to implement

1. **Identify effective reinforcers**: Observe what the student genuinely values (don't assume)
2. **Deliver immediately**: Reinforce within seconds of the desired behaviour
3. **Be specific**: Name exactly what the student did well ("Thank you for raising your hand")
4. **Be consistent**: Reinforce the behaviour every time initially, then gradually reduce frequency
5. **Vary reinforcers**: Rotate what you use to avoid satiation
6. **Fade to natural reinforcement**: Work towards naturally occurring consequences (e.g., getting needs met, experiencing success)

### Types of reinforcement

- **Social**: Praise, attention, smiles, high-fives
- **Tangible**: Preferred items, stickers, tokens
- **Activity**: Access to preferred activities, computer time, special roles
- **Sensory**: Activities that provide sensory input (swing, fidget, movement)

### Practical examples

- Specific praise: "I noticed you waited your turn - that was really patient"
- Token system: Earn points toward preferred activity
- Natural reinforcement: Student uses device to request snack and immediately receives it
- Activity reinforcement: Complete work earns 5 minutes of preferred computer activity
- Sensory reinforcement: Access to sensory corner after remaining seated during lesson

## Good to know

- **Individual differences**: What reinforces one student may not reinforce another
- **Avoid bribery confusion**: Reinforcement happens after behaviour; bribes are offered before
- **Quality over quantity**: Genuine, specific praise is more powerful than excessive generic praise
- **Natural reinforcement is ideal**: Work towards intrinsic motivation and naturally occurring consequences
- **Never withhold basic needs**: Food, water, breaks, safety are rights, not reinforcers

## Evidence base

Reinforcement is one of the most thoroughly researched principles in behavioural science with overwhelming evidence for effectiveness across all populations when implemented ethically and individualised appropriately.

**Related practices**: Differential Reinforcement, Functional Communication Training, Token Economy
