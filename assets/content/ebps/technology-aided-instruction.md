# Technology-Aided Instruction and Intervention

## What it is

Using technology (apps, software, devices, interactive platforms) to support learning, communication, and skill development. Technology can increase engagement, provide individualised instruction, and support independence.

## Why it matters

Many autistic students are highly motivated by technology. Educational technology can provide consistent instruction, immediate feedback, visual supports, and opportunities for repetition without social pressure.

## How to implement

1. **Identify learning goals**: Determine what you want to teach or support
2. **Select appropriate technology**: Choose tools matched to student's needs and abilities
3. **Teach how to use the technology**: Explicitly instruct on using the tool before expecting independent use
4. **Integrate into routine**: Embed technology use into regular activities
5. **Monitor progress**: Track whether technology is achieving intended outcomes
6. **Balance with other methods**: Technology supplements, not replaces, human interaction

### Types of technology

- **Educational apps**: Maths practice, literacy, social skills training
- **Communication devices**: AAC apps and devices for non-speaking students
- **Visual supports**: Digital schedules, timers, checklists
- **Video modelling**: Recorded demonstrations of skills
- **Virtual reality**: Immersive practice environments for social skills and transitions
- **Interactive whiteboards**: Engaging whole-class instruction
- **Learning management systems**: Organising assignments and providing structure

### Practical examples

- Maths app provides immediate feedback and adaptive difficulty
- AAC device supports functional communication throughout day
- Visual schedule app with auditory reminders for transitions
- Video modelling library showing daily routines and social skills
- VR scenarios for practising job interviews before real work experience
- Interactive story apps supporting literacy development

## Good to know

- **Not a replacement for humans**: Technology supports teaching; it doesn't replace relationships
- **Avoid over-reliance**: Balance technology with non-digital activities
- **Accessibility matters**: Ensure technology is genuinely accessible (font size, audio options, simple navigation)
- **Motivation can wane**: What's engaging initially may lose appeal; rotate tools

## Evidence base

Technology-aided instruction has growing research support, particularly for communication (AAC), academic instruction, and social skills training. Effectiveness varies by tool and implementation quality. Virtual reality shows promising emerging evidence.

**Related practices**: Augmentative & Alternative Communication, Visual Supports, Video Modelling, Virtual Reality
