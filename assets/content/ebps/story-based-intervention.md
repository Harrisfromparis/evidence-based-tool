# Story-Based Intervention

## What it is

Using stories - both reading existing stories and creating personalised narratives - to teach concepts, support understanding, and prepare for new situations. Story-based approaches include bibliotherapy, social narratives, and using literature for learning.

## Why it matters

Stories provide safe, indirect ways to explore emotions, social situations, and challenges. Many students connect with story characters and situations, making stories powerful teaching tools.

## How to implement

1. **Select or create appropriate stories**: Match stories to student's interests, reading level, and learning goals
2. **Read together**: Share stories with student, allowing discussion and questions
3. **Discuss explicitly**: Talk about characters' feelings, choices, and consequences
4. **Connect to student's life**: Help student see parallels between story and their own experiences
5. **Use for preparation**: Stories can introduce new situations before they occur
6. **Create personalised stories**: Write stories about student's specific situations (see Social Narratives)

### Types of story-based interventions

- **Bibliotherapy**: Using existing children's literature to address emotional or social topics
- **Social narratives/stories**: Personalised stories about specific situations (see Social Narratives EBP)
- **Comic strip conversations**: Visual story formats showing social interactions
- **Experience stories**: Writing stories about events that happened to the student

### Topics addressed through stories

- Understanding and managing emotions
- Friendship and peer relationships
- Changes and transitions
- Problem-solving
- Perspective-taking
- Coping with challenges
- Understanding social expectations

### Practical examples

- Reading picture book about starting school before transition
- Using story about feelings to discuss emotional recognition
- Creating comic strip conversation showing social misunderstanding to process it
- Experience story written together about successful field trip
- Bibliotherapy using book about child with similar challenges

## Good to know

- **Match to comprehension level**: Stories must be developmentally appropriate
- **Discussion is key**: Simply reading without processing misses learning opportunity
- **Visual support helps**: Pictures, photographs, and drawings enhance understanding
- **Representation matters**: Seek autistic characters written by autistic authors when possible

## Evidence base

Story-based interventions have moderate research support for teaching social understanding, emotional concepts, and preparing for new situations. Personalised social narratives (see Social Narratives EBP) have stronger evidence than general bibliotherapy.

**Related practices**: Social Narratives, Video Modelling, Cognitive Behavioural Intervention
