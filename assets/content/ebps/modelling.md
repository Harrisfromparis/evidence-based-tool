# Modelling

## What it is

Demonstrating a skill or behaviour so that students can observe and imitate. Modelling involves showing rather than telling, allowing students to see exactly what is expected.

## Why it matters

Many students, particularly autistic learners, benefit from clear, visual demonstrations rather than verbal instructions alone. Seeing a skill performed provides concrete understanding of expectations and techniques.

## How to implement

1. **Choose what to model**: Identify the specific skill or behaviour to demonstrate
2. **Gain attention**: Ensure the student is watching before you begin
3. **Demonstrate clearly**: Perform the skill slowly and clearly, emphasising key steps
4. **Think aloud**: Narrate your actions if appropriate ("First, I pick up the pencil")
5. **Invite imitation**: Give the student opportunity to try the skill immediately
6. **Provide feedback**: Acknowledge efforts and guide corrections

### Types of modelling

- **Live modelling**: Adult or peer demonstrates in person
- **Video modelling**: Student watches recorded demonstration (see Video Modelling EBP)
- **Self-modelling**: Student watches video of themselves successfully performing skill
- **Peer modelling**: Classmates demonstrate skills or behaviours

### Practical examples

- Teacher models how to use graphic organiser before asking students to complete one
- SNA demonstrates greeting routine at classroom door
- Student watches video of peer washing hands properly
- Teacher models how to ask for help politely
- Peer demonstrates science experiment procedure

## Good to know

- **Break complex skills down**: Model one step at a time for multi-step tasks
- **Avoid over-narration**: Too much talking during demonstration can be distracting
- **Allow processing time**: Some students need time to internalise what they've seen before attempting it
- **Repeat as needed**: Multiple demonstrations may be necessary for skill acquisition

## Evidence base

Modelling is a well-established teaching method with strong research support across all populations. For autistic learners, modelling is particularly effective when demonstrations are clear, concrete, and repeatable.
