# Social Narratives (Social Stories)

## What it is

Short, personalised stories that describe a situation, skill, or concept in a supportive, reassuring way. Social narratives help students understand social expectations and prepare for new or challenging situations.

## Why it matters

Many autistic students benefit from explicit, written information about social situations. Social narratives reduce anxiety, clarify expectations, and provide scripts for navigating complex social environments.

## How to implement

1. **Identify the situation**: Choose a specific situation the student finds challenging or needs to prepare for
2. **Write from student's perspective**: Use first-person ("I") and present tense
3. **Include descriptive sentences**: Explain what happens and why
4. **Add perspective sentences**: Describe what others may be thinking or feeling
5. **Include directive sentences**: Suggest helpful responses (not demands)
6. **Keep it brief**: 5-10 sentences for younger children, longer for adolescents
7. **Review with student**: Read together before the situation occurs

### Practical examples

- Narrative about fire drill: what will happen, why it happens, what to do
- Story about group work: what others expect, how to participate
- Narrative preparing for substitute teacher
- Story about managing disappointment when plans change
- Narrative explaining lunch routine in secondary school

## Good to know

- **Positive tone**: Social narratives should be supportive, never shaming
- **Avoid demands**: Use suggestions ("I can try...") not commands ("I must...")
- **Include visuals**: Photos or drawings often enhance understanding
- **Co-create when possible**: Student input makes narratives more relevant
- **Review multiple times**: Reading once is rarely sufficient

## Evidence base

Social narratives have moderate to strong research support, particularly for preparing students for new situations, teaching social skills, and reducing anxiety. Effectiveness increases when narratives are individualised and reviewed multiple times.

**Related practices**: Video Modelling, Cognitive Behavioural Intervention, Visual Supports
