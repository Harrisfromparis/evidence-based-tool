# Self-Management

## What it is

Teaching students to monitor their own behaviour, set goals, track progress, and self-reinforce. Self-management builds independence and internal control rather than reliance on external supervision.

## Why it matters

Self-management promotes autonomy, self-awareness, and lifelong skill development. Students who can monitor and adjust their own behaviour are better prepared for independence in school, work, and life.

## How to implement

1. **Choose target behaviour**: Select one specific, observable behaviour to self-monitor
2. **Teach self-observation**: Help student recognise when they are doing the target behaviour
3. **Introduce monitoring system**: Use checklist, tally, or app to track behaviour
4. **Set goals collaboratively**: Student participates in deciding what to aim for
5. **Teach self-reinforcement**: Student learns to recognise and celebrate their own success
6. **Fade adult involvement**: Gradually reduce adult monitoring as student becomes reliable

### Practical examples

- Student uses checklist to monitor completion of morning routine steps
- Anxiety scale (1-10) to monitor stress levels and request breaks
- Student tallies number of times they participate in class discussion
- Self-monitoring app tracks time spent on-task during independent work
- Student rates own focus at end of each lesson and identifies helpful strategies

## Good to know

- **Start with success**: Choose behaviours the student can realistically self-manage
- **Visual systems work best**: Concrete monitoring methods are more effective than abstract reflection
- **Student buy-in essential**: Self-management only works if student sees value in it
- **Accuracy matters**: Initially check that student's self-monitoring is accurate
- **Celebrate the process**: Reinforce the act of self-monitoring, not just perfect performance

## Evidence base

Self-management has strong research support for diverse outcomes including academic engagement, social skills, and emotional regulation. Studies show particular effectiveness for autistic learners when systems are visual, concrete, and individualised.

**Related practices**: Self-Regulation, Cognitive Behavioural Intervention, Visual Supports
