# Speech-Generating Devices (SGDs)

## What it is

Electronic devices that produce speech output when the user selects symbols, words, or letters. SGDs provide voice to individuals who are non-speaking or have unreliable speech.

## Why it matters

SGDs enable functional communication for individuals without reliable speech, supporting participation in education, social relationships, and community life. Having a voice - even a synthetic one - transforms access and agency.

## How to implement

1. **Assessment**: Speech and language therapist assesses communication needs and device access methods
2. **Select appropriate device**: Choose based on motor skills, language level, and support needs (dedicated device vs. tablet with app)
3. **Program vocabulary**: Start with core words and personalised vocabulary
4. **Teach device use**: Explicitly instruct on navigation and message construction
5. **Model constantly**: Adults must use the device whilst speaking to demonstrate its use
6. **Respond immediately**: Honour all communication attempts to build motivation
7. **Maintain and update**: Keep device charged, available, and vocabulary current

### Types of SGDs

- **Dedicated devices**: Purpose-built communication devices (Tobii Dynavox, Proloquo, TouchChat)
- **Tablet-based**: iPad or Android tablets with AAC apps (more affordable, versatile)
- **Single-message devices**: Simple buttons for specific messages

### Access methods

- **Direct selection**: Touching symbols on screen
- **Scanning**: Device highlights options; user selects via switch
- **Eye gaze**: User looks at symbols to select them

### Practical examples

- Student uses iPad with Proloquo2Go to request materials, comment, ask questions
- Non-speaking student uses eye-gaze device to participate in class discussions
- Student uses single-message button to greet peers at beginning of day
- Tablet with core vocabulary board available on desk throughout day

## Good to know

- **Not a "last resort"**: SGDs should be introduced early, not after years of speech therapy failure
- **Doesn't prevent speech**: Many users develop some speech whilst using device
- **Always available**: Device must be within reach at all times, like a speaking person's voice
- **Requires team support**: SLT, family, and school must collaborate on implementation

## Evidence base

SGDs have strong research evidence showing improved communication, increased social interaction, reduced challenging behaviours, and often emergence of speech. Most effective when modelled consistently and integrated throughout the day.

**Related practices**: Augmentative & Alternative Communication, Functional Communication Training, Technology-Aided Instruction
