# Functional Communication Training

## What it is

Teaching students more effective ways to communicate their needs, particularly when current communication methods (including challenging behaviours) are not working well. FCT provides acceptable alternatives that serve the same function.

## Why it matters

Many challenging behaviours occur because students lack effective ways to communicate needs. When we teach functional communication, we honour the student's need to express themselves whilst providing a method that works better for everyone.

## How to implement

1. **Identify the function**: Use FBA to understand what the student is communicating through current behaviour
2. **Choose an alternative**: Select a communication method the student can reliably use (words, signs, pictures, device, gestures)
3. **Teach the alternative**: Model and practise the new communication method
4. **Respond immediately**: When student uses the alternative, honour the communication right away
5. **Reinforce consistently**: The new communication must be more effective than the old behaviour

### Practical examples

- Student hits to escape demands → Teach "break" sign or card
- Student screams for attention → Teach to tap adult's arm or use "help" button on device
- Student grabs items from peers → Teach "Can I have it?" using visual card
- Student elopes when overwhelmed → Teach to point to "quiet space" visual

## Good to know

- **The alternative must work**: If we don't respond to the new communication, students will revert to what worked before
- **Start simple**: Choose communication methods within student's current ability
- **Respond to all attempts**: Reinforce even imperfect efforts to use new communication
- **Consider capacity**: During high distress, students may not be able to use complex communication

## Evidence base

FCT is one of the most researched and effective interventions for reducing challenging behaviours by teaching functional communication alternatives. Success rates are highest when communication alternatives are simple, accessible, and consistently honoured.

**Related practices**: Functional Behaviour Assessment, Augmentative & Alternative Communication, Differential Reinforcement
