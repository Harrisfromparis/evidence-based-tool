# Self-Regulation

## What it is

Supporting students in managing their own emotional, sensory, and behavioural responses. Self-regulation involves recognising internal states, understanding what supports regulation, and implementing strategies independently.

## Why it matters

Self-regulation is essential for independence, wellbeing, and lifelong functioning. Students who can recognise and manage their own regulatory needs are better equipped for school, work, relationships, and life.

## How to implement

1. **Teach self-awareness**: Help students recognise their internal states (emotions, sensory needs, energy levels)
2. **Identify triggers and patterns**: What situations challenge regulation?
3. **Build strategy toolkit**: Teach multiple regulation strategies
4. **Practise when calm**: Rehearse strategies before they're needed
5. **Support strategy use**: Prompt and reinforce when student uses regulation strategies
6. **Create supportive environment**: Reduce unnecessary regulatory demands

### Regulation strategies to teach

- **Sensory**: Movement breaks, fidgets, deep pressure, quiet space
- **Cognitive**: Self-talk, perspective-taking, problem-solving
- **Emotional**: Naming feelings, using calming strategies, seeking support
- **Physiological**: Deep breathing, progressive muscle relaxation, exercise
- **Environmental**: Removing from triggering situation, reducing demands temporarily

### Practical examples

- Student uses anxiety scale to monitor stress and request breaks before overwhelm
- Visual emotion chart helps student identify current feeling and select coping strategy
- Student independently takes movement break when noticing restlessness
- Calm-down corner with sensory tools student can access without permission
- Student uses self-talk strategies practised with adult: "I can handle this" / "I'll ask for help"

## Good to know

- **Co-regulation comes first**: Adults must help regulate before students can self-regulate
- **Not just "calm down"**: Telling students to regulate without teaching how is ineffective
- **Individual strategies**: What regulates one person may dysregulate another
- **Ongoing need**: Self-regulation is not "graduated from"; it's a lifelong process
- **Environmental modification helps**: Making environments less dysregulating reduces need for constant self-regulation effort

## Evidence base

Self-regulation interventions have strong research support showing improvements in emotional control, behavioural regulation, and academic engagement. Approaches combining self-awareness, strategy instruction, and environmental support show best outcomes.

**Related practices**: Self-Management, Cognitive Behavioural Intervention, Sensory Integration, Antecedent-Based Intervention
