# Scripting

## What it is

Providing pre-written phrases, sentences, or conversational sequences that students can use in social situations. Scripts give students language to use when spontaneous generation is difficult.

## Why it matters

Many autistic students know what they want to communicate but struggle to generate appropriate language in the moment. Scripts provide reliable language tools, reducing anxiety and supporting successful social interaction.

## How to implement

1. **Identify situations**: Which social situations cause difficulty or anxiety?
2. **Co-create scripts**: Work with student to develop useful phrases
3. **Keep scripts functional and flexible**: Provide templates, not rigid word-for-word requirements
4. **Make scripts accessible**: Written on cards, stored in phone, posted visually
5. **Practise scripts**: Rehearse in low-pressure situations before real use
6. **Support generalisation**: Help student adapt scripts across similar situations
7. **Fade when appropriate**: Some students naturally move beyond scripts; others use them long-term

### Common scripted situations

- Greeting peers and adults
- Asking for help or clarification
- Joining group conversations or activities
- Declining unwanted social interactions politely
- Responding to common questions
- Making small talk
- Disagreeing respectfully
- Asking someone to stop doing something

### Practical examples

- Card with greetings: "Good morning" / "How are you?" / "See you later"
- Script for asking to join game: "Can I play?" / "What are you playing?" / "That looks fun"
- Template for asking for help: "I need help with ___" / "Can you explain ___?"
- Script for when overwhelmed: "I need a break, please" / "This is too loud for me"
- Phone notes with conversation starters and responses

## Good to know

- **Not robotic**: Scripts are tools for communication, not enforced rigidity
- **Student voice matters**: Scripts should reflect student's natural communication style
- **Flexibility is key**: Teach adaptable phrases, not memorised speeches
- **Some students script naturally**: Self-generated repeated phrases serve similar function
- **Long-term use is fine**: Relying on scripts is not a problem; it's an effective accommodation

## Evidence base

Scripting has moderate research support as part of broader social communication interventions. Studies show scripts reduce anxiety, increase successful social interactions, and support communication in challenging situations. Evidence is stronger when scripts are individualised and taught within social skills training programmes.

**Related practices**: Social Skills Training, Social Narratives, Video Modelling, Visual Supports
