# Picture Exchange Communication System (PECS)

## What it is

Structured AAC system teaching functional communication through exchanging pictures for desired items or activities. PECS follows a specific six-phase protocol, beginning with simple requesting and progressing to more complex communication.

## Why it matters

PECS provides a concrete, visual communication method for individuals with limited or no functional speech. The system builds independent communication initiation, which is critical for functional interaction.

## How to implement

PECS follows a specific protocol with six phases:

**Phase 1: How to Communicate**
Student learns to exchange single picture for desired item

**Phase 2: Distance and Persistence**
Student learns to get communication book, remove picture, approach communication partner, and exchange

**Phase 3: Picture Discrimination**
Student learns to select correct picture from multiple options

**Phase 4: Sentence Structure**
Student builds simple sentences using sentence strip ("I want" + item)

**Phase 5: Responding to Questions**
Student learns to answer "What do you want?"

**Phase 6: Commenting**
Student learns to comment spontaneously, not just request

### Critical implementation elements

- **Two trainers initially**: One physical prompter, one receiver
- **Immediate reinforcement**: Student immediately receives requested item
- **Student initiation**: Student must initiate exchange; trainer doesn't prompt
- **Errorless teaching**: Physical prompts prevent errors
- **Systematic progression**: Don't move to next phase until current phase is mastered

### Practical examples

- Student hands teacher picture of "biscuit" and immediately receives biscuit
- Student goes to communication book, selects "play" picture, and gives it to adult
- Student builds sentence "I want swing" using sentence strip with two pictures
- Student answers "What do you want?" by selecting and exchanging appropriate picture

## Good to know

- **Requires specific training**: PECS trainers should attend official training workshops
- **Not just picture pointing**: PECS specifically involves exchanging pictures to communication partner
- **Can transition to device**: Many students later transition from PECS to SGD or app
- **Doesn't prevent speech**: Research shows PECS often supports speech development

## Evidence base

PECS has strong research support for teaching functional communication, particularly requesting, in individuals with limited speech. Studies show improvements in spontaneous communication and, in many cases, emergence of speech. PECS is most effective when implemented with fidelity to the protocol by trained practitioners.
