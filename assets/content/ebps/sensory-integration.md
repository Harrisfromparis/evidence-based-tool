# Sensory Integration

## What it is

Addressing sensory processing differences through environmental modifications, sensory diets, and therapeutic activities that support sensory regulation and comfort.

## Why it matters

Many autistic individuals experience the sensory world differently - some sensations are overwhelming, others are under-registered, and many seek specific sensory input. Addressing sensory needs reduces distress and supports engagement.

## How to implement

1. **Assess sensory profile**: Observe what sensory input the student seeks, avoids, or is sensitive to
2. **Modify environment**: Adjust lighting, noise, seating, visual clutter based on sensory needs
3. **Provide sensory tools**: Offer fidgets, weighted items, noise-cancelling headphones, movement breaks
4. **Create sensory diet**: Plan regular sensory activities throughout day
5. **Respect sensory preferences**: Honour student's sensory needs as valid accommodation
6. **Occupational therapy consultation**: Work with OT for individualised sensory strategies

### Sensory systems to consider

- **Visual**: Lighting, visual clutter, colour, movement
- **Auditory**: Background noise, sudden sounds, volume, pitch
- **Tactile**: Touch, textures, temperature, clothing
- **Proprioceptive**: Body awareness, pressure, resistance
- **Vestibular**: Movement, balance, position changes
- **Olfactory**: Smells (food, cleaning products, perfumes)
- **Gustatory**: Taste, food textures, oral sensitivities
- **Interoceptive**: Internal body sensations (hunger, toileting, pain)

### Practical examples

- Providing noise-cancelling headphones during loud activities
- Dimming fluorescent lights or allowing sunglasses indoors
- Offering wobble cushion or exercise ball for movement needs
- Creating quiet sensory corner with dim lighting and soft textures
- Allowing fidget tools during seated work
- Weighted blanket or lap pad for calming proprioceptive input
- Movement breaks every 30 minutes for sensory-seeking students
- Removing tags from clothing or allowing preferred comfortable clothes

## Good to know

- **Sensory needs are real**: Not behavioural choices or preferences to indulge
- **Accommodations, not rewards**: Sensory supports should be available as needed, not earned
- **Individual differences**: What helps one student may distress another
- **Occupational therapists are experts**: Consult OT for complex sensory needs

## Evidence base

Sensory integration approaches have moderate research support when implemented by qualified occupational therapists. Environmental modifications and sensory accommodations have stronger evidence than intensive sensory therapy alone. Individualised sensory supports show effectiveness for reducing distress and improving participation.

**Related practices**: Antecedent-Based Intervention, Self-Management, Exercise and Movement
