# Video Modelling

## What it is

Students watch video recordings of target behaviours or skills being demonstrated, then imitate what they observed. Video modelling provides clear, repeatable demonstrations that students can review as needed.

## Why it matters

Video modelling allows students to see exactly what is expected without social pressure of live observation. Students can watch repeatedly, pause, and focus on specific details. Many autistic students are visual learners who benefit from this format.

## How to implement

1. **Identify target skill**: Choose specific behaviour or skill to teach
2. **Create or select video**: Record demonstration or use existing video (1-3 minutes typically)
3. **Keep it clear and simple**: Focus on the target skill without distractions
4. **Watch with student**: View together initially, discussing key points
5. **Provide practice opportunity**: Give student chance to perform skill immediately after viewing
6. **Review as needed**: Let student watch multiple times

### Types of video modelling

- **Basic video modelling**: Someone else demonstrates the skill
- **Video self-modelling**: Student watches video of themselves successfully performing the skill
- **Point-of-view video**: Camera shows first-person perspective of performing skill
- **Video prompting**: Video broken into individual steps, shown one at a time

### Practical examples

- Video showing proper handwashing technique
- Recording of peer inviting others to join playground game
- Video self-modelling of student successfully giving presentation
- Point-of-view video of navigating school lunch line
- Video demonstrating how to ask teacher for help
- Recording of appropriate group work behaviours

## Good to know

- **Quality matters less than clarity**: Professional production not required; clear demonstration is what matters
- **Shorter is better**: Brief videos (1-3 minutes) are more effective than lengthy ones
- **Repetition is helpful**: Watching multiple times enhances learning
- **Generalisation needs support**: Skills learned from video may need prompting in real situations initially
- **Self-modelling is powerful**: Seeing themselves succeed is highly motivating for many students

## Evidence base

Video modelling has strong research support across diverse skills including social skills, communication, daily living skills, and academic behaviours. Video self-modelling shows particularly strong effects. Research indicates video modelling is efficient and engaging for autistic learners.
