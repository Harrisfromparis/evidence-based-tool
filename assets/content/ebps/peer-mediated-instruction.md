# Peer-Mediated Instruction and Intervention

## What it is

Training typically developing peers to support and include autistic classmates through structured interactions, social opportunities, and learning activities.

## Why it matters

Peer relationships are critical for wellbeing and belong to the student's natural environment. Peer-mediated approaches promote genuine inclusion, reduce reliance on adults, and create authentic social learning opportunities.

## How to implement

1. **Select peer partners**: Invite volunteers who are socially competent, kind, and reliable
2. **Train peers explicitly**: Teach specific strategies (initiating, prompting, reinforcing, turn-taking)
3. **Provide clear roles**: Give peers concrete tasks and expectations
4. **Support and supervise**: Adults monitor initially, fading support as peers become confident
5. **Reinforce peer efforts**: Acknowledge and thank peer partners for their involvement
6. **Rotate partners**: Involve multiple peers to avoid dependence on single relationships

### Practical examples

- Peer buddies during unstructured times (break, lunch, transitions)
- Peer tutors for academic support in small groups
- Lunch bunch or friendship groups with trained peer facilitators
- Peer partners for cooperative learning activities
- Playground buddies who invite inclusion in games

## Good to know

- **Voluntary participation**: Peers must choose to participate; never force involvement
- **Avoid exploitation**: Peer support should benefit both students, not place burden on peers
- **Natural interactions**: The goal is genuine friendship, not therapy relationships
- **Monitor peer wellbeing**: Ensure peers are not overwhelmed or taking on inappropriate responsibility
- **Celebrate mutuality**: Emphasise what autistic students bring to friendships, not just what they need

## Evidence base

Peer-mediated approaches have strong research support for improving social skills, increasing social interactions, and promoting inclusion. Studies show benefits for both autistic students and peer partners, particularly when peers receive adequate training and support.

**Related practices**: Social Skills Training, Modelling, Naturalistic Intervention
