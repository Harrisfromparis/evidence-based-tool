# Social Skills Training

## What it is

Explicitly teaching social skills through instruction, modelling, practice, and feedback. Skills are broken down into concrete steps and practised in supportive contexts before being applied in natural settings.

## Why it matters

Many social skills that neurotypical children learn through observation must be explicitly taught to autistic students. Structured social skills training provides the explicit instruction and practice opportunities needed for skill development.

## How to implement

1. **Assess needs**: Identify which specific social skills would benefit the student
2. **Break skills into steps**: Make implicit social rules explicit and concrete
3. **Teach directly**: Explain the skill, why it matters, and when to use it
4. **Model and demonstrate**: Show what the skill looks like in action
5. **Practise in safe setting**: Role-play and rehearse before expecting use in real situations
6. **Provide feedback**: Give specific, constructive guidance on practice attempts
7. **Support generalisation**: Help student use skills in natural contexts with prompts and reinforcement

### Skills commonly taught

- Greetings and farewells
- Taking turns in conversation
- Reading facial expressions and body language
- Asking for help appropriately
- Responding to teasing
- Making and maintaining friendships
- Group work collaboration

### Practical examples

- Small group practising conversation skills with adult facilitation
- Role-playing how to join playground games
- Video modelling demonstrating appropriate greetings
- Practising recognising emotions from photographs
- Rehearsing scripts for common social situations

## Good to know

- **Not about forcing conformity**: Teaching skills gives options; using them remains the student's choice
- **Respect autistic social preferences**: Some autistic social behaviour is not wrong, just different
- **Generalisation is challenging**: Skills learned in training often don't automatically transfer to real situations
- **Peer involvement helps**: Practising with peers is more authentic than adult-only instruction
- **Functional relevance matters**: Prioritise skills the student actually needs and wants

## Evidence base

Social skills training has moderate research support when implemented with explicit instruction, modelling, practice, and generalisation programming. Effectiveness improves when training occurs in natural contexts with peer involvement.

**Related practices**: Peer-Mediated Instruction, Video Modelling, Social Narratives, Modelling
