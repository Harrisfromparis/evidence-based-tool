# Functional Behaviour Assessment

## What it is

Systematic process of understanding why a behaviour is occurring by identifying its function (what the student gains from it). FBA views behaviour as communication and focuses on understanding rather than simply stopping behaviours.

## Why it matters

Without understanding why a behaviour occurs, interventions often fail or make situations worse. FBA helps us see behaviour as meaningful communication about unmet needs, leading to more effective and respectful supports.

## How to implement

1. **Define the behaviour**: Describe what the student does in observable, specific terms
2. **Gather information**: Use ABC (Antecedent-Behaviour-Consequence) data, interviews, observations
3. **Identify patterns**: When, where, and with whom does the behaviour most often occur?
4. **Determine the function**: What does the student gain? (attention, escape/avoidance, access to items/activities, sensory input)
5. **Develop hypothesis**: Create a theory about why the behaviour serves a purpose
6. **Design intervention**: Address the underlying need, teach alternative communication

### The four main functions of behaviour

- **Attention**: Seeking interaction, connection, or notice from others
- **Escape/Avoidance**: Getting away from demands, sensory input, or social situations
- **Access**: Gaining preferred items, activities, or sensory experiences
- **Sensory**: Meeting internal sensory or regulatory needs

### Practical examples

- Student hits others during loud activities → Function: Escape from sensory overwhelm
- Student repeatedly asks same question → Function: Seeking reassurance (attention)
- Student damages materials → Function: Accessing preferred "clean-up" routine
- Student rocks during lessons → Function: Sensory regulation (not problematic)

## Good to know

- **Behaviour is communication**: Every behaviour serves a purpose, even if it's not immediately obvious
- **Multiple functions possible**: A behaviour might serve different functions in different contexts
- **No behaviour is "attention-seeking"**: Seeking connection is a valid human need
- **Address the need, not just the behaviour**: Stopping a behaviour without addressing the underlying need leads to substitute behaviours

## Evidence base

FBA is the gold standard for understanding and addressing challenging behaviours. Research consistently shows that interventions based on functional understanding are more effective and ethical than those that ignore the behaviour's purpose.

**Related practices**: Functional Communication Training, Antecedent-Based Intervention, Differential Reinforcement
