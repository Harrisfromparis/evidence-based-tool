# Prompting

## What it is

Providing cues or assistance to help students perform a skill they are learning. Prompts are temporary supports, gradually faded as the student becomes more independent.

## Why it matters

Prompts help students succeed whilst learning new skills, preventing frustration and repeated errors. Strategic use of prompts, followed by systematic fading, builds independence.

## How to implement

1. **Choose prompt type**: Select the least intrusive prompt that will ensure success
2. **Deliver prompts quickly**: Prompt before the student makes an error
3. **Fade systematically**: Gradually reduce prompts as student demonstrates capability
4. **Avoid prompt dependence**: Don't allow students to become reliant on maximum prompts
5. **Reinforce independent responses**: Provide strongest praise when student performs without prompts

### Prompt hierarchy (least to most intrusive)

1. **Natural cue**: Environmental or routine-based reminder (timer, visual schedule)
2. **Indirect verbal**: General reminder ("What do we do now?")
3. **Direct verbal**: Specific instruction ("Pick up the pencil")
4. **Gestural**: Pointing, nodding, demonstrating
5. **Model**: Showing exactly what to do
6. **Partial physical**: Light touch or guidance
7. **Full physical**: Hand-over-hand assistance

### Practical examples

- Visual checklist on desk prompts student through morning routine
- Teacher points to materials needed for task (gestural prompt)
- SNA models first step of multi-step task
- Light tap on shoulder reminds student to raise hand instead of shouting
- Hand-over-hand guidance for handwriting formation (faded quickly)

## Good to know

- **Start with least intrusive prompts**: Use only as much support as needed
- **Fade promptly**: Don't allow students to become prompt-dependent
- **Time delay strategy**: Wait progressively longer before prompting to encourage independence
- **Errorless learning**: For some skills, maximum prompts initially prevent discouragement

## Evidence base

Prompting strategies have extensive research support when combined with systematic fading procedures. Evidence shows prompting is most effective when matched to student need and reduced strategically.
