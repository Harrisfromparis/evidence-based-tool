# Pivotal Response Training (PRT)

## What it is

Naturalistic teaching approach targeting "pivotal" skills (motivation, self-initiation, responding to multiple cues) that lead to widespread improvements. PRT follows the child's lead and embeds learning in play and natural activities.

## Why it matters

Rather than teaching many individual skills, PRT targets foundational abilities that unlock broader development. The child-led, play-based approach is engaging and respectful whilst promoting genuine learning.

## How to implement

1. **Follow the child's lead**: Let the child choose activities and materials
2. **Create opportunities**: Arrange environment so child has reason to communicate or engage
3. **Provide clear instructions**: Give simple, direct cues
4. **Share control**: Take turns and give child choices throughout activity
5. **Reinforce attempts**: Respond to any effort, not just perfect responses
6. **Use natural reinforcers**: The reward is directly related to the behaviour (asking for toy = receiving toy)
7. **Intersperse maintenance tasks**: Mix mastered tasks with new learning to build confidence

### Key components of PRT

- **Child choice**: Student selects activities and materials
- **Turn-taking**: Adult and child share control
- **Natural reinforcement**: Consequences logically relate to behaviour
- **Reinforcing attempts**: Responding to good tries, not only correct responses
- **Multiple exemplars**: Teaching with variety to promote generalisation

### Practical examples

- During play, student reaches for blocks; adult labels colours and shapes as child builds
- Student shows interest in book; adult reads and pauses for student to request "more" or turn page
- Child attempts to request snack; adult responds immediately even if request is imperfect
- Student chooses craft activity; adult embeds following instructions and vocabulary teaching

## Good to know

- **Requires skilled implementation**: PRT takes training and practice to do well
- **Balances structure and play**: Maintains learning goals whilst feeling like natural interaction
- **Strong family component**: Parents are often trained to use PRT at home
- **Evidence for broader outcomes**: Improvements often extend beyond targeted skills

## Evidence base

PRT has strong research support showing improvements in communication, social skills, play, and reduction in challenging behaviours. Studies show PRT is particularly effective when parents are trained as interventionists. Outcomes often generalise well to untrained settings.

**Related practices**: Naturalistic Intervention, Parent-Implemented Intervention, Functional Communication Training
