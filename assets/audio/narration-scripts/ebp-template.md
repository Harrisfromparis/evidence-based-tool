## Format

Each EBP narration follows this structure:

---

## [EBP Name]

[Clear, concise definition in plain language]

[Explanation of importance and impact]

[Step-by-step implementation guidance]

### Practical examples
[2-3 concrete Irish educational examples]

[Key ethical or practical points to remember]

[Brief summary of research support]

[Names of related EBPs]

## Sample: Visual Supports

**Visual Supports**

**What it is**

Visual supports use pictures, symbols, written words, objects, or videos to support understanding, communication, and independence. Visual supports make abstract concepts concrete and provide information that remains visible over time.

**Why it matters**

Many autistic individuals are strong visual learners. Spoken language is transient and must be processed quickly. Visual supports remain available for review and reduce reliance on auditory processing and working memory.

**How to implement**

First, identify where the student struggles with understanding, remembering, or organising. Then, choose an appropriate visual format: pictures, photographs, symbols, written words, objects, or video. Place supports where the student can easily see and use them. Teach the student how to reference the visual support. Review regularly, as what helps may change over time.

**Practical examples**

A photo schedule showing the daily class routine. A visual timer showing time remaining for an activity. A first-then board showing "First maths worksheet, then computer time." Visual rules posted in the classroom. Labelled bins for materials and belongings. A transition countdown strip showing 5, 4, 3, 2, 1, transition.

**Important considerations**

Visual supports are not infantilising when the format is age-appropriate. They benefit all ages. Some students prefer photos, others prefer symbols or words. Keep visual supports available - don't remove them too quickly. Independence may require ongoing visual support. Visual supports work best when used consistently across all environments.

**Evidence base**

Visual supports have extensive research support as one of the most effective practices for autistic learners. Studies show improvements in communication, organisation, task completion, behaviour, and independence.

**Related practices**

Visual supports are closely related to structured work systems, self-management, social narratives, and augmentative and alternative communication.

---

**Note**: Narration should be clear, measured, and professional. Pause between sections. Estimated duration per EBP: 90-120 seconds.
